# Remediation Governance and POA&M Lifecycle

## Purpose
Translate Day 7 simulated assessment findings into owned, prioritized corrective actions. This is a training scenario, not an approved production POA&M.

## Workflow
1. Validate each finding against authoritative evidence and confirm affected assets/CUI boundary.
2. Assign accountable system owner, action owner, risk owner, and independent verifier.
3. Prioritize by severity **and** exposure, exploitability, mission impact, and compensating controls; severity alone is not an authorization decision.
4. Define milestones, dependencies, resourcing, due dates, and objective closure evidence.
5. Implement fixes through approved change management; record tickets and configuration changes.
6. Independently retest using appropriate assessment procedures; record outcome and reviewer.
7. Close only when evidence supports correction; otherwise reopen or document formally approved risk treatment.
8. Escalate overdue critical/high items to security leadership and system owners.

## Decision rights
- **Action owner:** executes remediation and supplies evidence.
- **Control owner:** validates operating procedure and documentation.
- **GRC lead:** maintains traceability, quality, and reporting.
- **Independent assessor:** evaluates closure evidence and retests.
- **Authorizing / risk acceptance official:** decides on residual risk within delegated authority; no approval is assumed.

## Cadence (proposed)
Weekly remediation review; monthly leadership summary; immediate escalation for material changes. All target dates remain TBD pending management approval.

## Traceability
POAM ID → Day 7 Finding ID → Control Test ID → Evidence → Independent Retest → Closure Decision.

## Standards note
Mappings to NIST SP 800-53, NIST SP 800-171, and CMMC must be validated for the applicable revision and contract. Do not present an illustrative POA&M as a CMMC assessment submission.
